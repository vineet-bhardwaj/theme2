Our Founding Congregation

The Patrician Brothers
— teachers by vocation.

Founded in Ireland in 1808 by Bishop Daniel Delany, the Brothers of St. Patrick have carried one conviction across five continents: that education, offered freely and with affection, is the surest way to lift a young life. St. Patrick’s Academy, Meerut, is part of that story.

The Founder

A bishop who began with a Sunday school

Daniel Delany returned to an Ireland where Catholic schooling was barely permitted and most children grew up unable to read. He did not begin with a building or an endowment — he began with a Sunday school in Tullow, County Carlow, taught by volunteers he trained himself.

From that work grew two congregations: the Brigidine Sisters in 1807, and in 1808 the Brothers of St. Patrick — the Patrician Brothers. Their purpose was plain and it has not changed: to teach the young, especially the poor, and to do it with patience, discipline and genuine warmth.

In India

A long innings on Indian soil

The Brothers came to India in the 1870s and never left. Today their schools, hostels and outreach centres run from Tamil Nadu to Uttar Pradesh — many of them in towns where a good school was, and often still is, the only ladder available to a child.

St. Patrick’s Academy, Meerut, opened in 1965 on land that was then largely orchard. Six decades later the founding intent is unchanged: rigorous academics, an open door, and a house culture that teaches children to look after one another.
